================================================================================
RebalanceIQ
A Bike-Share Demand Forecasting and Fleet Rebalancing Decision System
================================================================================

Course       : Applied Data Science (ADS) - Interim Project
Framework    : BADIR (Business question, Analysis plan, Data collection,
               Insights, Recommendations)
Final output : Integrated single-source-of-truth dataset, models, interactive
               decision dashboard, and BADIR report


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Bike-share systems constantly become unbalanced. In the morning, bikes flow out
of residential neighbourhoods into business districts; in the evening the flow
reverses. Some stations run empty (customers cannot start a ride) while others
fill up (customers cannot return a bike). Every empty or full station means
lost rides, lost revenue and frustrated customers. Operators fix this by moving
bikes with vans and trikes, but rebalancing crews and vehicles are expensive
and limited.

RebalanceIQ integrates official Citi Bike trip and station data with weather,
calendar, public-event, subway-ridership and census data into one unified
dataset, and uses it to:
  1. explain what drives bike demand at each station and hour,
  2. forecast hourly pickups and drop-offs per station,
  3. predict which stations are at risk of running empty or full,
  4. recommend which bikes to move, from where, to where and when, within a
     daily rebalancing budget,
  5. estimate the operational and financial impact (rides recovered, cost,
     net value) compared with simpler strategies.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Business problem
  Citi Bike (New York City) operates more than 2,000 stations. Demand varies
  strongly by hour, day, season, weather, location and nearby activity.
  Operations managers need to know:
    - How many bikes will be picked up and returned at each station in the
      coming hours?
    - Which stations will run out of bikes or docks, and when?
    - Where should the limited rebalancing fleet move bikes to recover the
      most rides for the money spent?

Why it matters
  - An empty station loses every ride that would have started there.
  - A full station blocks returns, creating poor customer experience and
    extra trips for riders.
  - Rebalancing resources (staff hours, vans, trikes) are limited, so they
    must be directed where they create the most value.

Primary business objective
  Maximize net value per day:
      revenue from rides recovered by rebalancing
    - cost of rebalancing moves
  within a fixed daily operating budget.

Policy relevance
  Bike-share is part of New York City's public transport and sustainability
  strategy. Results also inform city planners (NYC DOT) on where station
  capacity or bike-lane investment would reduce chronic imbalance.

5. SINGLE SOURCE OF TRUTH (SSOT): DATA INTEGRATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 Integrated data model

  The SSOT is a star schema stored as Parquet files (or a DuckDB / PostgreSQL
  database):

    fact_trips             one row per trip (cleaned S1)
    fact_station_hour      one row per station per hour (MAIN ANALYTICAL TABLE)
    dim_station            one row per station (S2 + S7 + S8 + S9 + S10 + S5
                           locations)
    dim_time               one row per hour (S4 + S11)
    dim_event              one row per event (S6), mapped to nearby stations

5.2 Identifiers and join logic

  Join                          Key / method
  ----------------------------  -----------------------------------------------
  Trips -> stations             trip start_station_id / end_station_id matched
                                to GBFS short_name; unmatched IDs resolved by
                                station name and coordinates (nearest station
                                within 50 m)
  Station status -> stations    GBFS station_id
  Trips / status -> time        timestamp truncated to the hour
                                (America/New_York time zone)
  Weather -> time               NOAA observation time rounded to the hour;
                                multiple readings in one hour averaged
  Subway -> stations            spatial join: subway station complexes within
                                500 m of each bike station; ridership summed
                                per station-hour
  Events -> stations            event location geocoded; events within 1 km of
                                a station and active during the hour counted
  Bike lanes -> stations        total bike-lane length within 500 m (spatial)
  Census ACS / LODES -> stations station point-in-polygon to census tract
                                (TIGER/Line); LODES jobs summed within 500 m
  Holidays / school days -> time calendar date

5.3 Schema and unit consistency

  - All timestamps converted to America/New_York local time (the status
    archive is stored in UTC); daylight saving
    transitions handled explicitly.
  - Distances in kilometres (haversine from latitude / longitude), durations
    in minutes, temperature in degrees Celsius, precipitation in millimetres,
    wind speed in km/h, money in US dollars.
  - Consistent snake_case column names and data types across all tables.
  - Station IDs stored as strings (to keep formats such as "6140.05").

5.4 Duplicate handling

  - Duplicate ride_id values removed.
  - Duplicate status snapshots (same station, same timestamp) removed.
  - Duplicate weather records (multiple report types per hour) resolved by
    priority rule (routine hourly report first).
  - Stations that were renamed or relocated tracked with valid-from / valid-to
    dates.

5.5 Data-quality checks

  - Completeness: % missing per column, per source; missing hours in weather
    and status series.
  - Validity: trip duration between 1 minute and 24 hours; coordinates inside
    NYC bounding box; capacity > 0; non-negative counts.
  - Consistency: end time after start time; bikes + docks available not
    exceeding capacity; station-hour pickups equal trip counts from fact_trips.
  - Outliers: abnormal trip durations, speeds, and station counts flagged.
  - Reconciliation: monthly trip totals compared with Citi Bike Monthly
    Operating Reports.
  - All checks implemented as automated tests and summarised in a data-quality
    report.

5.6 Main analytical table: fact_station_hour (40+ variables)

6. BADIR FRAMEWORK APPLIED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 B - BUSINESS QUESTION

  "Given a limited daily rebalancing budget, which stations should receive or
   give up bikes, how many, and when, to maximize rides served and net value?"

  KPIs
    - Rides served and estimated rides lost (unmet demand)
    - Station empty-minutes and full-minutes
    - Rebalancing cost per day and per bike moved
    - Net value of rebalancing (revenue recovered - cost)
    - Forecast accuracy (MAE, RMSE) and risk-prediction quality (precision,
      recall)


6.2 A - ANALYSIS PLAN

  Hypotheses (tested statistically)
    H1  Weekday demand peaks at commute hours; weekend demand peaks midday
        (compare hourly profiles; t-test / ANOVA).
    H2  Rain and low temperatures reduce ridership; the effect is stronger for
        casual riders than members (regression with interaction; t-test).
    H3  Member vs. casual usage differs by station type and time
        (chi-square test of independence).
    H4  Stations near subway stations and dense employment show stronger
        commute-time imbalance (correlation / regression).
    H5  Large public events increase nearby demand (difference in means,
        event vs. comparable non-event hours).
    H6  A small share of stations accounts for most empty/full minutes
        (Pareto analysis).
    H7  More bike-lane coverage is associated with higher station demand.

  Analysis steps
    1. Data integration into the SSOT and data-quality report.
    2. Exploratory data analysis and visualisation (time, space, rider type).
    3. Hypothesis testing (H1 - H7).
    4. Station segmentation by demand profile (clustering).
    5. Demand forecasting (regression and time-series models).
    6. Empty / full risk prediction (classification).
    7. Rebalancing recommendation and impact simulation.


6.3 D - DATA COLLECTION AND PREPARATION

  - Download and load all sources S1 - S12 (scripts for each source, logged).
  - Clean each source (Section 5.5) and integrate into the SSOT (Section 5).
  - Feature engineering: calendar features, lag and rolling features, weather
    categories (dry / light rain / heavy rain), spatial features, cluster
    labels.
  - Unmet-demand estimate: for hours when a station was empty, expected
    demand is estimated from comparable hours when it had bikes available.
  - Time-based train / validation / test split (e.g. train Jan - Sep,
    validate Oct, test Nov - Dec). No random shuffling of time-series data.


6.4 I - INSIGHTS (METHODS AND MODELS)

  Descriptive analytics
    - Hourly, daily, weekly and seasonal demand patterns.
    - Net-flow maps (sources vs. sinks) for morning and evening peaks.
    - Member vs. casual, e-bike vs. classic usage.
    - Pareto analysis of problem stations.

  Station segmentation (clustering)
    - K-means (and hierarchical clustering for comparison) on normalised
      24-hour weekday / weekend demand profiles and station features.
    - Expected clusters: residential, business district, tourist / leisure,
      transit hub, mixed.
    - Cluster quality: elbow method and silhouette score.

  Demand forecasting (regression / time series)
    Target: pickups and drop-offs per station per hour.
    Models compared
      - Baselines: historical average for the same hour and weekday;
        seasonal naive (same hour last week).
      - Time series: exponential smoothing / SARIMA at cluster level.
      - Linear and regularised regression (Ridge / Lasso).
      - Tree-based models: decision tree, random forest, gradient boosting.
    Evaluation: MAE, RMSE, MAPE (on non-zero hours), time-based
    cross-validation, error broken down by cluster and hour.

  Empty / full risk prediction (classification)
    Target: will the station be empty (or full) within the next 2 hours?
    Models: logistic regression, decision tree, random forest, gradient
    boosting.
    Evaluation: confusion matrix, precision, recall, F1, ROC-AUC; threshold
    chosen by cost of missed stockouts vs. unnecessary truck visits.

  Explainability
    - Regression coefficients and feature importance.
    - SHAP values for tree-based models: global drivers (hour, weather,
      station type, events) and station-level explanations, e.g.
        "Station W 52 St & 6 Ave likely empty by 9 am: weekday commute peak,
         dry weather, 3,000 jobs within 500 m, current stock 4 bikes."


6.5 R - RECOMMENDATIONS

  Recommendation 1: Daily rebalancing plan (core decision)
    For each planning window (e.g. 6 am, 3 pm, overnight):
      1. Projected bikes at each station = current bikes + forecast drop-offs
         - forecast pickups.
      2. Deficit stations: projected bikes below a safety level.
         Surplus stations: projected bikes above a maximum level
         (close to capacity).
      3. Value of each move = expected rides recovered x revenue per ride.
         Cost of each move = fixed cost per stop + cost per km + cost per bike.
      4. Greedy prioritisation: rank deficit stations by rides at risk; serve
         each from the nearest surplus station(s); repeat until the daily
         budget or fleet capacity is used.
    Output: list of moves - from station, to station, number of bikes, time
    window, expected rides recovered, cost, net value.

  Recommendation 2: Station-level structural actions
    - Stations that are chronically empty or full -> add docks, add a nearby
      station, or set up a "valet" / corral service at peak times.
    - Input for NYC DOT on bike-lane and station-network planning.

  Recommendation 3: Rider-incentive opportunities
    - Identify trips where rewarding riders to return bikes to deficit
      stations would be cheaper than van moves.

  Recommendation 4: Operational scheduling
    - Crew shift planning based on when and where imbalance builds up.

8. SIMULATION AND FINANCIAL IMPACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Simulation method
    1. Replay test-period days hour by hour using actual demand (observed
       trips plus estimated unmet demand).
    2. Track bikes at every station; a pickup succeeds only if a bike is
       available, a return only if a dock is available.
    3. Apply each strategy's rebalancing moves at the planned times.
    4. Record rides served, rides lost, empty/full minutes, bikes moved, cost.

  Financial assumptions (adjustable in the dashboard)
    - Revenue per ride: casual and member values derived from Citi Bike's
      published pricing (single ride, per-minute e-bike fees, annual
      membership spread over average member rides).
    - Rebalancing cost: van cost per hour, crew cost per hour, bikes per van
      load, average speed in traffic.

  Outputs
    - Rides recovered vs. no rebalancing and vs. baselines
    - Cost per ride recovered
    - Net value per day and annualised
    - Sensitivity analysis (pessimistic / expected / optimistic) on revenue
      per ride, cost per move and budget level


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. ASSUMPTIONS AND LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Demand during empty periods is not observed and is estimated.
  - Rebalancing costs are not published; values are assumptions with
    sensitivity analysis.
  - Travel distance between stations uses straight-line distance with a
    road-network factor, not exact routing.
  - Weather from one central station is applied city-wide.
  - Census data is 5-year average and does not change hourly.
